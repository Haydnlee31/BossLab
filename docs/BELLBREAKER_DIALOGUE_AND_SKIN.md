# Bellbreaker voice and skin revision

The smith speaks at encounter start, when his bell cracks and on defeat. His ordinary swings leave room for the furnace, steps and metal impact. An active phrase stops when a higher-priority line starts or the encounter resets. The three cues are static Unreal assets. The scripts, casting direction, raw performance, mastered takes and hashes are in `artifacts/bellbreaker-dialogue` and `unreal/art-studio/source/bellbreaker`. They come from one original Qwen3-TTS voice-design performance. A live stereo opening and the complete recorded encounter mix are in `artifacts/unreal/bellbreaker-dialogue`. Runtime logging confirmed all three loaded and the opening line played. The recordings matched the opening to its source 1.42 s into one fight and the phase line to its source 2.72 s into a separate phase-two run. The defeat line is included in the dry audition.

The fitted skeletal mesh had weights crossing the empty space between the torso and the arms. A raised elbow dragged apron and sleeve faces out of shape. The replacement repaints arm and torso weights, duplicates 124 seam vertices and relinks 171 polygon corners to separate the underarm from the garment. The split preserves the original exterior polygons, resting surface positions, UVs, skeleton and materials. A final lining adds 786 interior triangles using the same leather atlas, bringing the rendered mesh to 92,450 triangles. The sibling Unreal asset keeps the original available for rollback. The animation solve also preserves segment lengths and limits the total wrist bend relative to the reference pose. The visible boss selects the repaired mesh when installed. The collision capsule and combat timings use the same engine definitions.

The exact 132-degree arm-raise comparison uses the same 85,816 nondegenerate triangles before and after. Faces stretched beyond twice their original area fell from 4,239 to 691. Faces squeezed below half area fell from 1,254 to 662. Those are measures of a deliberately extreme pose. In-game capture uses the rendered four-attack sequence, a fixed side camera, and sampled skinned triangles from the active LOD. Unreal can reorder triangles on import, so index-stride samples are descriptive and cannot establish a per-face improvement. Source and audit details are in `artifacts/unreal/bellbreaker-dialogue/skin-repaint.json`.

The headless FBX import hit a Slate assertion. The graphical editor reported a successful save and exit code 1 on shutdown. A fresh Unreal process subsequently loaded the specific ForgeSkin asset and captured every attack. Both the original and corrected skin used LOD 0 throughout the sampled attack poses. The full 600-frame clip was encoded and decoded. The left hand can still miss its target by up to 8.7 cm; both shockwave contacts stayed within 0.01 cm horizontally of the intended blast centre.

On the rendered Bell Quake at age 82, the opt-in surface diagnostic matched all 91,664 triangles from an intermediate skin to the final seam correction by their resting corners. At the foreground cuff, a face which stretched to 25.510 times its resting area measured 1.040 after the seam fix. A second changed from 17.992 to 1.093. Of 85,816 well-sized triangles in that frame, 363 still stretched to over ten times their rest area, down from 776. Pinching near the shoulder remains visible. See `quake-comparison.jpg` for the original/repaired view, `quake-cuff-review.jpg` for the measured cuff, and `quake-surface-comparison.json` for the exact sample. No deformation-free claim.

The phase-break comparison at 13.60 seconds shows the window previously visible through the sleeve replaced by a continuous leather interior. See `sleeve-lining.jpg`. The final 20 s capture decoded 600/600 frames and the dedicated forge suite passed 2/2 with the lined asset. The native suite on the compiled gameplay code passed 54/54.
