Owner identifies excessive bulk. Re-read current status/execution/decisions/claims and inspected V7 hero, front and side against the approved concept direction. Silhouette/proportions now take priority over materials. Preserve character height, sword size, camera, lighting and previous evidence. ## Where the bulk comes from

The shoulder stack and mantle create a broad horizontal mass; widely separated lames float out from the upper arm. Upper-arm cylinders and cuff bands remain almost uniformly thick. The cuirass projects strongly in profile and terminates in a flared fauld/belt cylinder. Wide parallel skirt panels conceal leg taper and knee articulation. Together these forms read as a bulky suit rather than a fitted knight. The head is not the principal problem; shrinking it substantially would exaggerate body bulk.

The approved target has a clearer shoulder-to-waist convergence, narrower layered shoulder support, tapered forearms and cloth that follows legs with unequal folds. The following are reversible design trial deltas relative to V7, not exact anatomical measurements recovered from a perspective raster.

Do not multiply every transform indiscriminately. Move articulation centers only where necessary for layer fit; preserve body height and limb lengths. Fit straps, mail borders, hidden cloth and closures to the revised regional surfaces. Keep the same sword beside the figure so scale remains assessable.

## First actual V8 review

Inspected actual hero/front/side/clay_hero. Waist and shoulder width are visibly reduced; helmet scale remains sensible. Do not narrow waist further. The coordinated change exposes disproportionate upper-arm/elbow cuff mass and can-like forearm profile. Agreed bounded local elbow fan/cuff/sleeve reduction around the original joint center, approximately15–18%, preserving functional joint cap and clearance. Fit all dependent bands/liners. Reassess upper-arm radius before any optional further reduction; avoid making limbs emaciated to chase a narrow torso.

Shoulder stack now shows tooth-like bright offsets; seat successive lower lames inward along the arm curve and reduce side gaps rather than shrinking their tips independently. Belt/pouch anchors must follow new waist. Floating skirt binding/hem curves are a confirmed regression and require group-fit correction. Cloth still reads as parallel boards; shape lower control rows and outer taper rather than applying another uniform scale. No material changes or overall acceptance.

## Third-fit proportion review

Inspected updated hero/front/side. Arm/cuff balance improves and floating skirt bindings are no longer visible. Waist should remain at this point; further compression would overemphasize the remaining hardware. Boots now look bulbous against slimmer legs. Directed one coordinated footwear correction: width about8–10% less and upper instep height6–8% less, blending to zero change at sole/contact and ankle attachment. Preserve foot length, toe clearance, heel and floor placement; fit toe lames/sole/borders together. No head or leg shrink.

Overall silhouette is visibly leaner than V7. Stepped shoulder outlines, broad simple cloth boards and smooth materials remain below the target; this proportion milestone does not close the global art gate.

## Final footwear and clay review

Inspected final boot-fit hero, front and side, then coherent clay front, clay side and material back. The footwear is now better balanced with the narrower legs. No conspicuous new toe-lame, ankle, floor-contact, skirt-piping or cape breakthrough appears in these reviewed views. This is visual inspection of static images, not a complete intersection or deformation test.

The material and clay views both show a visibly leaner silhouette: reduced shoulder spread, less barrel depth, a clearer chest-to-waist transition, and more balanced arms and footwear. The head retains sensible scale and the sword remains a stable comparison. This addresses the owner's specific bulk criticism more directly than the preceding material-only trials.

**Verdict: useful proportion milestone; overall artistic target remains unmet.** The shoulder stack still has regular stepped/tooth-like corners, the front skirt hangs as stiff projecting boards, and broad smooth armor fields, simplified hands and combed mantle remain conspicuous. No production anatomy, rig, cloth motion, runtime performance or reference-quality approval follows from this increment. Materials were intentionally held stable.
