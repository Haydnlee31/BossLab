## Recommendation: A, the Ossuary Gaoler

Choose the fitted steel and barred skull visor of A. Its profession is readable before ornament: a jailer who has become his own prison. The open face bars, lean waist, left chain mechanism and right straight sword provide four clear identifiers. Retain the player's fitted, repaired construction language but distinguish the boss through exposed dead anatomy, vertical bars and cold blue witnesses rather than another embroidered red cape.

B's ivory mask/crown makes a strong ceremonial alternative, but its wide mourning mantle hides casting shoulder, feet and recovery; it also repeats our unresolved large-cloth problem. C communicates undeath immediately but numerous linen strips and exposed bones compete with the chain and require extensive skinning. Borrow only C's restrained linen/tendon exposure at the Gaoler's neck and elbow. Do not combine all three costumes.

For the first actual design, shorten A's split coat to just below the knee, divide front/back at the hips and leave feet visible. Avoid hanging keys or secondary chains across the attack silhouette. Keep the left reel visibly heavier, but seat it around the forearm with an open elbow and clear wrist exit. The sword-side shoulder stays compact enough for a thrust. The barred visor must reveal an actual skull volume from side and three-quarter views; no painted skull on a flat mask.

## Original narrative made physical

Proposed lore, subject to owner approval: the Warden once kept three condemned witnesses alive until judgment. The court died first. He continues to retrieve prisoners and pronounce sentences while the witnesses remember what he cannot.

- **The Last Sentence, straight sword:** a narrow, stiff thrust-capable blade, modest straight guard, dark leather grip and weighted pommel. Three shallow witness notches belong on the ricasso, away from the cutting edge. A polished thrust point and worn guard/thumb contact explain use. Keep the player sword's seven-oath identity separate. The sheet's label “execution sword” describes fantasy purpose; it does not justify a blunt execution-style tip for this thrust mechanic.
- **The Return, chain and hooked manacle:** a reel fixed by a broad leather-backed forearm cuff, visible axle/guide mouth and one legible terminal hook. The cuff is the anchor, not a floating cage strapped to fingertips. A broken manacle suggests retrieval, but its open jaw must resolve into one visible hook tip matching the combat sweep. Wear concentrates inside guide rings and link contact. No gratuitous spikes or giant invisible grab volume.
- **The Three Witnesses, blue-flame skulls:** one intact brow, one broken jaw and one missing crown corner give distinct silhouettes using a shared base. Their flame is supernatural; the bone remains readable and mostly non-emissive. They remember accusation, testimony and verdict—not three hidden autonomous enemies. This lore does not assign passive damage, separate targeting or adaptive behavior.

The proposed sequence tells that story: Return casts, the Warden visibly takes the chain's load, releases the prisoner, then raises Sentence for an avoidable thrust while Witnesses fall silent. Preserve the combat proposal's explicit release/escape window. Never let a dramatic execution pose imply a guaranteed follow-up or override engine timings.

## Shape, material and effect hierarchy

Use three dominant masses: narrow barred head, fitted rib/waist armor, asymmetrical chain forearm. Keep the coat subordinate. Steel fields are blackened grey with limited silver contact edges; arming cloth is charcoal, burial linen dirty warm grey, skull bone desaturated ivory. Restrict old brass to axle, one chest seal and fasteners. Blue is reserved for skull fire and an explicit ability tell; do not make every armor crack glow.

At the arena camera, skulls form an unequal arc behind and outside the head/shoulders, never directly over the hook hand, blade, player or roll lane. During a chain tell they settle away from the casting arm. Begin with passive skull presentation; projectile attacks remain later explicit abilities. Disable all flame/bloom to check silhouette first. One restrained shared lighting contribution at most is a proposal to benchmark, not a promised performance budget.

## Production gates that prevent another procedural-detail loop

1. **Shared construction sheet:** front, side, back and casting three-quarter use one torso/arm envelope. Draw the actual rear cuirass fastening, underarm opening, reel axle/exit, skull interior and coat attachment. Specify proportions against the existing boss skeleton/capsule; do not enlarge reach through cosmetics. Owner silhouette choice precedes detailed production.
2. **Real clay body and three poses:** build a connected weighted base, fitted hollow plates and short sewn panels. Review idle, maximum chain cast and deep reel brace from front/side/gameplay camera. Elbow bends, clavicle motion, sword grip, wrist exit and leg clearance must work before adding rivets. A static front render is insufficient. Rigid plate attachment can be a labeled prototype, not accepted cloth/skin deformation.
3. **One complete focal sample:** barred skull visor plus reel/hand assembly, at gameplay distance and close view. Bars need actual depth, skull cavities need coherent hidden surfaces, and the reel must have an uninterrupted chain exit. If it reads as stacked primitives, rebuild the control cage/section landmarks; do not add filigree, more fibers or noisy shader masks.
4. **Proper surface authoring:** unwrap visible plates/cloth into coherent islands; inspect checker stretching and overlaps as well as audits. Author one small steel/leather/bone/linen material sample under neutral and arena light. Require visible construction-driven finish, not global cloud noise or copied front-image shadows. Only propagate a sample that looks convincingly distinct at the intended viewing scale.
5. **Full action proof:** same asset in normal-speed throw, miss retrieval, tension, pull, released-player stab, interruption and death. Verify feet carry weight, hips/spine lead arms, cloth does not block legs, and chain never appears attached before accepted contact. Review with effects off, then restrained skulls on. Engine/QA owns contact, escape and cancellation gates; art owns correspondence and readability.

Keep old assets and fixed rehearsal available. Use one DCC writer and existing storage guards; no downloads, paid services, new cloth solver experiment, AI, RL or model adapter work. Current free-space headroom is not sufficient evidence that a new build/render is safe. These are proposed gates, not passing results or an implementation start.
