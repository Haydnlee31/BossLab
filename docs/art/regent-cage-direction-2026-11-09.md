## Initial handoff

Keep selected B: elongated funerary face inside a close hood, thin crown, lean rib torso, left chain forearm/right sword and a long shoulder-supported mantle. First produce neutral front/side/back plus face/shoulder crop without effects or surface detail.

The body must use shared connected cross-sections at neck, ribcage, waist and pelvis, with explicit shoulder loops flowing into upper arm, elbow and forearm. Side silhouette must retain actual lower-arm volume and a planted sole. Do not union open shells or hide disconnected joints beneath plates. Record topology as supporting evidence, then judge the actual surface.

The garment must have a yoke crossing shoulder and nape, joined rear gores above the hips and curved side gores wrapping the flank. Its front opening frames the rib torso; its lower mass remains ankle-length with restrained flare. This is a continuous three-dimensional garment volume assembled from panels, not four vertical strips, a flat rear board or an inflated cone. Establish attachment-led broad fold paths in the cage before density or noise.

Face landmarks must be actual surface: projecting nose bridge/tip, brow/eye sockets, sloping cheek fields and tapered chin within the hood opening. Avoid eye dots on a capsule and intersecting decorative planes. Crown points/embroidery/skulls are secondary until face, shoulder/torso and long garment silhouette read as B.
