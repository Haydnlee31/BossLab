## Panel and attachment contract

Use real separate pattern pieces, connected at declared seams rather than one weighted cone:

| Piece | Attachment and free region | Required behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Curved shoulder yoke | Supported across both shoulders over the padded vest; reinforced front tabs meet the anatomical-right clavicle clasp | Turns over the shoulder into the back, with no floating horizontal tab. Left deltoid/casting shoulder stays exposed enough to read the coil. Clasp carries cloth, not chain tension. |
| Paired rear gores | Sewn to yoke, joined at centre back above the hips; concealed opening below hips | Long rear hem remains near the ankle, above the sole with no train. Centre opening accommodates deep steps; broad gravity folds retain weight rather than an inflated waist dome. |
| Two front falls/stoles | Sewn to yoke, lightly retained at waist; separate free lower lengths | Unequal lower-calf-to-ankle hems frame the ribs. Anatomical-left fall remains medial to the elbow/reel route. Front falls never bind both legs or bridge the casting wrist. |
| Side gores | Join front/back cloth below arm openings; concealed splits allow hip separation | Maintain side coverage at rest, open under stride. A visible lining supplies depth when openings spread; no invisible torso or painted black hole. |
| Central mourning panel and divided under-robe | Panel supported at belt; independent leg openings beneath | Panel can lift/shift between separated legs without pulling the rear mantle. Belt attachment stays behind belt hardware. No skirt welded to both thighs. |
| Hood and nape drape | Cloth supported by head/neck region, nape rests over yoke | Mask/crown use an independent hidden harness. Looking down/twisting must not force jaw through ribs or drag the entire mantle with the head. |

Retain long cloth when solving fit: change seam placement, local ease, split opening or weighting first. Any proposed hem shortening beyond the selected uneven ankle silhouette needs explicit review; do not silently substitute A's coat. Gold embroidery belongs to the pattern pieces and bends with them.

## Chain arm and rig requirements

Anatomical left carries the reel; anatomical right holds the pointed straight sword. The reel has two seated leather-backed forearm straps, a visible guide mouth and a chain route extending past the wrist. Keep wrist flexion and elbow compression clear. Establish the route using the actual hook/link envelope plus an explicit recorded visual clearance allowance; do not guess a tiny nominal gap from a concept drawing.

For each pose, show the left elbow sweep and chain route with cloth visible, then with cloth hidden. No cloth triangle may bridge the arm opening or cross the launch/tension route. The reel remains attached to the forearm while shoulder/clavicle/spine contribute to reach; it must not float into place. Hook contact/line endpoints come from future engine state, not from animation anticipating contact.

The future body must be continuously skinned through shoulder, elbow, hip and knee. Plates have localized weights or appropriate rigid weighting; long cloth needs separate weighted regions/panel controls. A rigid cape attached to one spine bone is not an accepted solution. Begin with authored pose deformation, not physical cloth simulation. Full clips later require transition checks: six clean stills alone cannot prove deformation between them.

## Six required pose gates

These define visual acceptance, not timing authority. The consolidated proposal's release-before-stab rule remains intact. Tell/pull durations are untested hypotheses; do not change engine timing to fit an animation.

| Pose | Whole-body construction | Accept only if |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cast preparation** | Rear foot plants; hips lower and rotate away, chest/clavicle follow; left elbow draws beside ribs. Sword stays distinct at right hip. | Left shoulder/elbow and hook hand read at gameplay distance with skull effects off. Front falls frame rather than cover ribs; hood/mask remain seated. Rear hem clears the planted heel. |
| **2. Committed full cast** | Pelvis/chest uncoil; left arm extends along the committed line, front leg braces. Right weapon stays outside that line. | Wrist guide and hook launch route are unobstructed in front and side views. Mantle opening accommodates the arm without a straight stretched sheet across elbow/armpit. No grip, reel or shoulder detachment. Aim cannot visually home after commitment. |
| **3. Contact-confirmed pull brace** | Knees flex and hips shift back; left hand/reel draws toward waist against taut chain; right elbow prepares the blade. | Weight visibly opposes tension through feet and torso. Cloth does not swallow the tension line or expose a missing body. Deep hip separation opens concealed slits, preserving long hems without leg/boot penetration. Show a player stand-in at safe standoff for composition only. |
| **4. Release and independent thrust preparation** | Chain loses tension and clears the attack lane; body deliberately reorients, right elbow draws back for thrust. | Player stand-in is visibly free before the thrust begins. Chain is not still attached as a cinematic restraint. Sword/hand silhouette is distinct from left reel and mantle. This is a readable separate threat, not a hidden guaranteed second hit. |
| **5. Thrust/deep-step extension** | Right arm extends with chest/hip drive; forward knee bends, rear leg supports recovery; no full-body teleport. | Guard clears wrist, mask clears gorget, ribs overlap coherently. Front/side/back openings accommodate the deepest step without shortening the costume. Blade stays outside own cloth/body; torso does not become stationary while only weapon moves. |
| **6. Miss retrieval/recovery** | Hook misses or cancels; line slackens, left arm gathers/reels while hips return over feet. Sword stays guarded. | Clear recovery silhouette differs from the tension brace. Slack chain does not cut through mantle or loop around legs; no instant snap to idle. Show both the widest retrieval excursion and settled end as two subviews of this gate. |

Later interruption/death/wall/reset branches must cancel visual tether with engine cancellation, restore player control and suppress the follow-up as specified by combat design. They are required before gameplay acceptance, but are not implemented or validated by these pose drawings.

## Consistency and evidence contract

Front, side, back and three-quarter must use the same head/shoulder/elbow/wrist/waist/hip/knee/ankle landmarks and actual boss scale reference. Record body height, shoulder span and sword/chain attachment locations once; do not independently scale or mirror each drawing. In front view the left reel appears on the viewer's right; rear view reverses its screen side naturally. Crown points, clasp, panel seams and three skulls must agree across views.

First clay evidence should include neutral front/side/back/three-quarter, all six poses at the arena camera, and diagnostic side/back views of full cast, pull brace and deep thrust. Add mantle-lifted and arm-route overlays as explicitly labelled diagnostics, not substituted beauty views. Use fixed cameras and visible floor contact; no cropped feet or flame obscuring errors. Record which poses/views were actually inspected.

Pass the construction gate only when attachments remain seated, long-panel identity survives, arm/stride openings work, and silhouette distinguishes cast/pull/release/miss with effects disabled. Failed views get exact object/pose annotations and targeted corrections. The consolidated stage plan permits initial candidate plus two targeted corrections per visual gate; a failed gate calls for a changed authoring method, not more ornament.

No tests, renders, meshes, weights, clips, solver results, material fidelity, performance or AI behavior are claimed by this document. No heavy job was run. Future implementation remains subject to the one-writer workflow, 10 GB Unreal budget and 24 GiB free-space guard.
