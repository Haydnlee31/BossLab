## 1. Build the mantle as a sewn garment before grooming

Retain v4's descending angular shoulder armor. Replace its rectangular carpet backing with a connected horseshoe yoke around the back of the neck, open at front. Deliberately author three landmark bands: collar anchor; shoulder/acromion support; unsupported dropped hem. These are not concentric planar rectangles. Surface between them follows the shoulder, then hangs below the support. Front hem drops about25mm over the shoulder; rear hem hangs40–60mm lower. One side extends30mm farther forward. These are reversible design dimensions, not physical simulation results.

Use two principal folds from each front fastening: a broad tension ridge toward the back neck and a deeper slack valley toward the outside hem. Give left and right different valley positions/depths. Avoid regular sine-wave corrugations. A continuous cloth surface with thickness and a turned hem is preferable to disconnected inflated pads. Put a narrow seam on the inner neck band and one joined-panel seam behind each shoulder. Their location should explain assembly. No random stitch stripes. Show backing-only clay from front, side and rear before fibers conceal it.

The pale exposed neck insert should become a fitted dark padded liner, with enough clearance from the gorget for believable layering. Do not solve it by scaling through armor or covering every gap with black geometry.

### Groom anchored to garment

Root flow must follow the actual yoke surface. Short8–15mm undercoat makes a continuous mass, densest over the backing and thinning at sewn borders. Guide flow starts near neck, travels outward over shoulder, then down/back with the unsupported hem. Author6–8 unequal dominant clumps per side, lengths30–55mm, with staggered overlapping roots. Clumps should emerge from undercoat rather than sit as identical isolated bundles on top. Vary their width, direction and silhouette role deliberately; do not merely randomize a grid.

Some long tips overlap hem and neighbors to break the hard carpet edge. Keep front center open so collar/strap reads. Avoid solid triangular leaf geometry, rows of talons, uniformly vertical brush strands or an evenly fuzzy ring. Mild asymmetry conveys worn fur; chaotic spikes do not. Confirm strand paths remain outside curved backing rather than disappearing through it.

**Milestone 1 acceptance:** in backing-only clay this reads as attached cloth with gravity folds; in fur material it reads as one irregular soft shoulder garment. The v4 rigid rectangular edge and repeated tile rows must visibly disappear. Preserve actual failed probes and inspect neutral front/side/back.

## 2. Fitted plate fields and garment craftsmanship

Keep angular descending lame corners and narrow exposed edges from v4. Give each shoulder plate shallow double curvature: wrapping horizontally around shoulder/upper arm and curving gently down from upper attachment toward free edge. Do not restore inflated oval domes. Shape the upper cap to the actual shoulder support rather than an arbitrary plane. Lower lames overlap by a readable consistent construction margin and leave enough negative space to distinguish layers without huge black gaps.

Use a modeled rolled perimeter/return on selected exposed plate edges, with small actual thickness, not universal bright wire trim. A few deliberately placed rivets/strap anchors explain retention. Existing gate plaque remains the central motif; no proliferation of symbols. Fit torso side panels to waist and underarm instead of adding appliqué to a blank cone. Avoid underlayer breakthroughs.

For cloth, prioritize a sewn split surcoat and cape attachment: seam line, folded hem, broad weight-driven folds and restrained edge abrasion. If cape remains static, say so. Do not label a modeled drape as simulated. Material wear belongs near real seams, strap pressure, plate overlap and handled edges. Broad surfaces should retain fine low-contrast variation and quiet areas. Avoid cloud mottling and scratches uniformly tiled across unrelated pieces.

**Milestone 2 acceptance:** actual medium view shows thin curved plates attached in a plausible order, visibly sewn garment borders and differentiated wool/leather/steel. Large forms retain priority. Side/back views must not reveal unsupported flat slabs or obvious penetration hidden by front lighting.

## 3. Glove anatomy and genuine seam-based UVs

After whole-figure progress, replace straight distal-palm ledge with landmark-driven surface. MCP root heights follow an arc; middle highest, index/ring slightly lower, little lowest. Palm hollow is broad/shallow, thenar pad connects to thumb through a saddle, wrist narrows. Finger roots grow out of palm instead of four equal pipes attached to a slab. Preserve unequal finger lengths and restrained relaxed flexion. Fit dorsal plates only after leather anatomy improves in clay.

Cut actual UV seams along glove side, wrist and finger-side lines as needed. Separate palm/dorsal/side islands; unfold finger tubes without collapsed side triangles. Verify nonzero face UV area and inspect a checker for stretching; disclose any overlaps. A named UV layer is not proof of a proper unwrap. Then place compression creases, grip polish and stitched seam margin on those islands. Existing projected masks may need repainting; do not retain them blindly on a new layout.

**Milestone 3 acceptance:** clay demonstrates shaped palm/web/knuckles; checker and UV sheet demonstrate useful islands; actual material crop shows seam/contact wear. No rig/grip/deformation certification from a static pose.
