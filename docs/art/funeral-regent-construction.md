## Identity that must survive every view

- Long indigo mourning mantle with aged gold architectural embroidery, unequal layered hems and a visible dark lining. Preserve its ankle-reaching ceremonial mass; do not turn it into a short jacket for convenience.
- A narrow ivory funerary mask with deeply recessed dark eyes, elongated cheek/jaw planes and a closed, still mouth. It is a fitted mask over a real head volume, not the A skull cage or barred visor.
- A slender open crown above the hood, with restrained narrow points and a continuous seated circlet. No large antlers, broad helmet dome or oversized crown spikes.
- Silver overlapping rib-like torso plates above a fitted dark waist. Keep these readable through the opening between the mantle fronts.
- Anatomical left forearm carries the chain reel; anatomical right hand carries the straight pointed sword. In a front view the chain appears on the viewer's right. Preserve this handedness in rear and pose drawings.
- Exactly three floating bone skulls with restrained blue flame; these sit outside the head/hand silhouettes. Their arrangement may move, but they do not become attached shoulder ornaments.

## Common construction and proportional landmarks

Use the same body and armor envelope for front, side, back and three-quarter. Establish head centre, nape, clavicles, shoulder joints, elbow/wrist, natural waist, hip, knee and ankle as shared horizontal guides. Existing boss skeleton/capsule remains the future scale reference; the sheet must not silently enlarge weapon reach or collision.

Keep a lean fitted ribcage and waist beneath the fabric. Crown points rise roughly one third of a mask height above its circlet; mask width is narrower than the hood opening. Shoulder plates follow the deltoid, rather than extending as wide horizontal wings. Front stole hems end at unequal levels between lower calf and ankle; the principal rear hem sits just above the sole, with no long trailing train. Exact clearances need later pose verification, not nominal millimetre promises from a concept image.

## Front: what attaches to what

The hood has a shaped crown seam and two side gussets, with enough thickness to frame the mask without swallowing its jaw. A narrow hidden leather harness around the head carries mask and crown; the cloth itself does not suspend the metal crown. The mask has interior depth, eye openings and an attachment route at temples/nape.

The mantle rests on a shaped shoulder yoke over the arming garment. Its anatomical right front is drawn diagonally toward a round clavicle clasp, while the left shoulder armor stays more exposed above the reel arm. This asymmetry matches B; do not mirror it accidentally. The clasp joins reinforced cloth tabs at the yoke, not a brooch floating on an uninterrupted flat sheet.

Below the clasp, two separate front falls frame the rib plates and waist. They attach at the shoulder/yoke and are lightly retained near the belt, with free lower lengths. Leave an actual opening for the right sword forearm and left casting elbow. The long central mourning panel is a separate belt-supported piece over the divided under-robe, not welded to both thighs. Embroidery follows these pattern pieces and is interrupted at seams/folds.

Build the silver ribs as overlapping shells on a dark padded vest: broad central sternum connection, descending lateral returns and a narrow waist termination. They wrap toward the side seam, not detached chevrons pasted onto a black rectangle. Collar and upper chest leave neck rotation possible.

## Side: depth and movement clearance

Show skull/head depth behind the mask, crown seated on its circlet, hood falling over the nape and a layered neck gap. The mask's chin does not collide with the first rib plate in a downward look.

The shoulder yoke turns continuously over the shoulder into the rear mantle; it must not become a flat board hovering behind the neck. Draw two or three load folds from clasp/shoulder attachments, then longer, quieter gravity folds below the waist. Distinguish cloth thickness and lining from plate returns. Avoid a balloon bulge behind the waist or a ruler-straight diagonal cape wedge.

The left reel lies along the forearm, anchored by two broad leather-backed straps with elbow and wrist left free. Draw the axle, spool body, guide mouth and one continuous chain route exiting past the wrist. The hook is a separate terminal piece. Keep the mantle outside the casting elbow path and the chain route outside cloth. The right sword grip/guard has real hand clearance; its pointed blade remains straight and thrust-capable.

Use concealed side openings in the mantle and divided under-robe to permit stepping and stance widening while retaining the long front/back silhouette. Do not solve clearance by cutting the whole garment to knee length or hiding the legs permanently inside a rigid cone.

## Back: unseen structure must be designed

Continue the fitted cuirass around the ribs into two rear plate fields with a central spine seam and restrained side fastenings beneath the mantle. Show this structure in a small mantle-lifted inset. The body does not disappear behind a textured back plane.

The hood ends in a shaped nape drape over the mantle's curved yoke seam. A broad rear cloth panel is supported across both shoulders, with centre-back seam or paired gores supplying actual width; a concealed centre-back opening begins below the hips for a deep step. The yoke sits on the upper torso, with no exposed horizontal rectangular tab. Side gores connect the rear drape to the front falls while retaining arm openings.

Place one large aged gold funerary arch on the rear panel, aligned to its centre seam, plus restrained border embroidery. Let folds interrupt the motif; do not lay a perfectly flat gold sticker over cloth. Worn hem damage follows seams and load points in a few areas rather than a uniformly saw-toothed edge. Rear crown points, arm hardware and skull count must match the front.
